This PhD is a theoretical approach to the study of cinematic time in interactive audiovisual works – a subject continuously transformed by the constant advance of information and communication technologies, which has modified the way we use and conceive time and space in moving images throughout history.

It is especially relevant now, as digital media are generating multiple temporal configurations strongly conditioned by new interactive interfaces, mobile devices and the Internet, which can be identified in works of cybertext, Future Cinema, digital art and videogames. The thesis establishes a new framework for analysing these interactive audiovisual temporalities from two perspectives: their narrative organisation, uses and temporal processing; and their experience and perception.

From the analysis of several case studies, the work provides a state of the art of digital temporalities; a theoretical map of interrelated areas and categories in the creation and reception of temporalities in digital cinematic images; elements and models for the analysis and design of interactive experiences based on temporal enjoyment; and a set of clarifying proposals for temporal concepts related to digital storytelling, which may be the starting point for new research in this field.

*Keywords: digital temporalities, cinematic time, temporal perception, interactive media, digital art, videogames, cybertext, phenomenology, new media.*
